Four Questions

You've arrived here without a path to follow. Choose the kingdom you're in, and the house will remember.

Ruby — Sol-Torren Sapphire — Nadir Haven Emerald — Noxglen Amethyst — Nocturna

A Question, Finally

with Ember

Ember turns the bow over once more in your hands before letting you keep it, then looks up at you properly for the first time since you arrived — not excited, for once, just genuinely curious.

"Alright. I have to ask. Why are you actually here?"

True, or not true

Every question the house has asked you so far — every if, every elif — was quietly asking one of two things underneath: True, or False. These are called booleans, and they're the simplest values in the language: a thing is either the case, or it isn't.

From the spellbook:
has_bow = True print(has_bow)
True

Comparisons — == for "is equal to," != for "is not equal to," along with >, <, >=, and <= — all produce a boolean too, once the house checks them.

From the spellbook:
arrows = 0 print(arrows == 0)
True

And when you need to ask more than one true-or-false question at once, three small words combine them: and requires both sides to be true, or only needs one, and not flips whatever follows it.

From the spellbook:
has_bow = True has_catalyst = False print(has_bow and has_catalyst) print(has_bow or has_catalyst) print(not has_catalyst)
False True True
Ember waits
