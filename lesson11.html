CHAPTER XI by candlelight

Four Homelands

You've arrived here without a path to follow. Choose the gem you carried, and the house will remember where it leads.

Ruby — Sol-Torren Sapphire — Nadir Haven Emerald — Noxglen Amethyst — Nocturna

Nadir Haven

the path of water

Virelia circles you once, tail free and easy again, and for the first time she smiles without any panic behind it. "You didn't have to do that," she says. "Come — let me show you where I'm from."

The moment the water closes over your head, you brace for the burn in your lungs that never comes. Your sapphire pulses faintly against your skin, and you realize, with some disbelief, that you're breathing. Nadir Haven opens up below you: a city grown into coral and drowned stone, lit by strings of glowing jellyfish.

Repeating until something changes

Virelia's people don't count their descents in fixed numbers — they sink until the pressure feels right, however long that takes. In the spellbook's tongue, that's a while loop: it repeats for as long as a condition stays true, rather than a number you chose ahead of time.

From the spellbook:
depth = 0 while depth < 5: print("You sink deeper.") depth = depth + 1
You sink deeper. You sink deeper. You sink deeper. You sink deeper. You sink deeper.

Be careful: if nothing inside the loop ever changes the condition, it never stops. The current has to shift a little each time, or you'd sink forever.

A spell that calls on itself

There's a second way to repeat something, stranger than the first: a function that calls itself, each time asking a slightly smaller version of the same question, until it reaches an answer it doesn't need to ask again. This is called recursion — think of it like an echo of your own voice, bouncing back smaller and smaller until it fades to nothing.

From the spellbook:
def count_down(n): if n <= 0: print("You surface.") else: print(n) count_down(n - 1) count_down(3)
3 2 1 You surface.

Every recursive spell needs a way out — the if that stops it from calling itself forever, the way count_down stops once n reaches zero.

Try it yourself. Write something that repeats — a while loop, a function that calls itself, or both. It's yours to shape; the current only asks that something in your spell truly repeats.

Sol-Torren

the path of fire

The flames along Autumn's arms fold back into her skin, fur and feather smoothing into something more human. "Well," she says, brushing soot from her sleeve like it's an inconvenience rather than a near-disaster, "I owe you for that. Come on — let me take you home."

Sol-Torren rises out of heat-shimmer on the horizon before it's anything more than a smudge: towers of red stone, rivers of something that glows instead of flows. Your ruby warms against your chest, and when a spark drifts too close to your hand, you feel nothing at all.

Noxglen

the path of earth

Aspen's shoulders ease, just slightly — not trust exactly, but something adjacent to it. "It's rare," he says, "to find a traveler in Fae lands who can actually back up their words." He looks you over once, unimpressed. "You can't save the world dressed like that. Come. Let's get you properly seen."

Noxglen unfolds around you slowly, like it doesn't quite trust straight lines — streets that curve for no reason, lanterns that burn without flame. Your emerald hums, and suddenly you can see it: threads of light woven through everything, every fae nearby outlined in something like color that isn't color. Aspen's is almost too much to look at directly.

Nocturna

the path of shadow

"You have nerve," he says, like it pains him to admit it. "Fine. Come see where nerve like that actually gets you." He doesn't wait to see if you follow.

Nocturna doesn't so much appear as it stops being hidden — a city built into a dark so total it swallows the edges of things. Your amethyst pulses once, and the dark peels back just for you: shapes, distances, the shifting edge of Buer's expression, all suddenly visible in shades of violet and grey.
