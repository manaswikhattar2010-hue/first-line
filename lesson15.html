CHAPTER XV by candlelight

Four Weapons

You've arrived here without a path to follow. Choose the weapon you carry, and the house will remember.

Ruby — the bow Sapphire — the catalyst Emerald — the twin blades Amethyst — the glaive

The Rune on the Bow

the path of fire

You notice it only once the forge-light catches the wood at the right angle — a line of script running the length of the bow's grip, too fine to have been carved by hand. Ember leans in beside you, squinting. "Huh. Never actually read that part."

Looking things up by name

A list holds things in order, one after another. A dictionary holds things by name instead — a label, called a key, paired with whatever it belongs to, called a value. Written in curly braces, each pair joined by a colon.

From the spellbook:
weapon = {"name": "glaive", "element": "shadow"} print(weapon["element"])
shadow

Rather than asking for the third thing in a row, like a list, a dictionary lets you ask for the thing named "element" directly — and it hands back whatever was paired with it.

The rune on your bow won't read itself aloud until it's properly indexed. Here is what it says:

The flames of your continuance will ever burn.

Try it yourself. Build a dictionary that holds this rune as one of its values — under any key you'd like to give it — and then access that key to speak it aloud with print().
