Eight Roads

Evelyn's Offer

the path of fire

Evelyn straightens slowly, rubbing at her wrists where the fire had scorched too close, and looks at you like she's reconsidering something.

"You didn't have to do that. Most people wouldn't have." She glances once at Autumn, then back to you. "My life's yours, then. In exchange, I'll take you to Nicole — and after that, I'll guide you the rest of the way up the Ridge myself."

Before you set out, she teaches you something to make sure you don't lose each other on the climb.

A spell that already knows where to look

Not every spell needs to be told everything twice. A function can carry a default value right in its own definition — something it falls back on if you don't give it anything else.

From the spellbook:
def locate(target="Nicole"): print(f"Searching for {target}...") locate() locate("the pearl")
Searching for Nicole... Searching for the pearl...

Called with nothing, locate() falls back to "Nicole" automatically. Called with something else, it uses that instead. The default is just what it reaches for when you don't hand it anything.

Try it yourself. Write a spell with a default value of your own, and call it once without telling it anything at all.

Evelyn watches the working settle with something like approval. "That'll do," she says. "Let's move — the Ridge doesn't get any warmer the longer we wait."
