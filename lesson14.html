CHAPTER XIV by candlelight

Four Kingdoms

You've arrived here without a path to follow. Choose the kingdom you entered, and the house will remember.

Ruby — deeper into Sol-Torren Sapphire — deeper into Nadir Haven Emerald — deeper into Noxglen Amethyst — deeper into Nocturna

The Heart of Sol-Torren

with Ember

Ember doesn't let you linger at the grove for long. "There's more to see," she says, already walking, "and I'd rather show you than describe it." She leads you past the tree line, deeper into the kingdom, toward towers you'd mistaken for the horizon.

A shorter way to weave words

You've been building sentences the long way so far — stitching words and values together with +. There's a cleaner way: an f-string, which lets you drop a value directly into the middle of your words, wrapped in curly braces.

The old way:
sparks = 3 print("Ember summons " + str(sparks) + " sparks.")
The f-string way:
f"Ember summons {sparks} sparks."
Ember summons 3 sparks.

The f right before the quotation mark tells the house to look inside for curly braces, and fill them in with whatever's between them — a variable, or even a small calculation.

Try it yourself. Write a spell using an f-string of your own — weave any value into any words you like.

at the heart of the kingdom

The towers open into a forge unlike anything you've seen — heat without smoke, flame that holds its shape like liquid glass. Ember pulls something long and dark from the coals, still glowing faintly along its grain.

An Ember-Wood Bow

Carved from a branch that burned but never fell, warm to the touch even now. It doesn't need arrows fletched with fire — it makes its own.

"Every hunter in Sol-Torren carries one," Ember says. "Now you do too."

The Heart of Nadir Haven

with Lorelei

Lorelei doesn't stay in the coral halls for long either. "You've barely seen anything yet," she says, already gliding deeper. She leads you down, past the last of the drifting jellyfish light, toward a glow that isn't coming from anything you recognize.

at the heart of the kingdom

The glow resolves into a chamber lined with drifting scrolls that never seem to touch the ground. Lorelei draws one book free from the current — its cover shifting color like oil on water, humming faintly against your hands the moment you take it.

A Tidebound Catalyst

A spellbook that writes down nothing on its own — but every working you cast near it seems to come to you a little easier, a little clearer, from now on.

"It won't cast anything for you," Lorelei warns. "It just... helps you remember how."

The Heart of Noxglen

with Briar

Briar rises from the window seat without being asked twice. "If my brother's letting you stay," he says, "you may as well see the rest of it." He leads you deeper into the castle, past halls that seem to rearrange themselves slightly whenever you're not looking directly at them.

at the heart of the kingdom

The hall finally opens into an armory carved straight from living root and pale stone. Briar picks through it without hesitation, like he already knew exactly what he was looking for, and sets two things down in front of you.

Twin Daggers & a Sterling Silver Sword

The daggers are light, quick, made for someone who'd rather not be seen at all. The sword is heavier, colder, unmistakably fae-make — the kind of blade that isn't meant to be hidden.

"One for when you'd rather not be noticed," Briar says, "and one for when you'd rather make sure you are."

The Heart of Nocturna

with Rhys

"Come, come," Rhys says, already moving before you've fully agreed to follow. "You didn't think the entrance hall was all there was to see, did you?" He leads you deeper into the palace, past doors that seem to close on their own the moment you pass them.

at the heart of the kingdom

The doors finally stop closing behind you, and the hall opens into a room with nothing in it but a single stand, lit by the same violet glow that seems to follow you everywhere in this kingdom. Rhys lifts something long and curved from it, testing its weight before offering it to you, hilt first.

A Nocturne Glaive

Curved like a scythe, black as the halls it came from, quiet in a way that has nothing to do with sound. It doesn't feel heavier the longer you hold it — if anything, the opposite.

"Do try not to lose that one," Rhys says, smiling in a way that doesn't reach his eyes. "It was rather difficult to acquire."
