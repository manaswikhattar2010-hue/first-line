Setting Off

the path of fire

Map restored, Nicole sends you off with a final set of numbers scrawled along the margin — elevations, distances, written as plain text rather than anything you can actually calculate with yet.

"Written like that, it's just decoration," Nicole says. "Turn it into a real number first, and the Ridge will make a lot more sense."

The same working as before

You've done this once already, back on the road here — type conversion, turning text into a true number with int(), or back into words with str(). The house still can't do math on a number dressed up as text.

From the spellbook:
elevation = "1200" elevation = int(elevation) print(elevation + 50)

The numbers resolve into something usable, and Evelyn's already waiting by the door, pack slung over one shoulder. "Diamond Ridge," she says, "doesn't wait for anyone. Let's go."

Searching the House

the path of earth

Aspen insists on searching before anything else, despite the burns still raw on his wrists. The bedroom tells its own story the moment you reach it — glass shattered across the floor, a window broken outward rather than in. Nicole didn't let something in. She went through it herself.

On the desk, a spellbook lies open to a page half-finished — a working mid-cast, abandoned, with a string of figures scrawled beside it in a hurried hand.

"Numbers," Celeste says, crouching over the page. "Written as text, not anything usable yet. Convert them, and we might know where she was going."

"Someone counted something," Lilith says, running her thumb over the marks. "Written like that, it's just scratches. Make it a real number, and maybe it tells us something."
